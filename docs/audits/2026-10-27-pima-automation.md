# Pima County reusable candidate collection

## Scope

The additive `az-pima-2026` family discovers explicitly dated, even-year November
general elections from the [county election index](https://www.pima.gov/elections).
An actually linked public event supplies the date. Official candidate and write-in
controls supply asset URLs. Filing, interest and primary-result lists do not
substitute for these publications. Future event dates and asset identifiers are
not generated from a year or a guessed pathname.

The current publications contain 104 nonjudicial ordinary candidate records and
12 write-ins suitable for this independent source. Ten individual executive
entries establish no joint-ticket pairing, and 14 judicial entries need a
separate retention contract. All 140 printed person claims are accounted for;
24 stay out of application choices. Blank write-in placeholders are not people.
The existing 207-record Pima/Yavapai composite, its corroborated joint tickets
and its default priority remain unchanged.

## Access and policy evidence

Ordinary requests reached the county index, its actually offered privacy and
disclaimer notices, and the `content.civicplus.com/api/assets/az-pimacounty/`
publications. Exact reviewed same-origin policy redirects remain constrained.
The county's 20-second request spacing and the content host's public asset
permission are retained. Policy-link or substantive notice changes require
review before document collection. No challenge solving, proxy fallback, TLS
weakening or access-control circumvention is configured.

The reviewed notices concern visitor privacy, accuracy limitations, external
links and official use of the county seal. No restriction on collecting these
candidate facts was identified in those notices. The seal, source artwork,
embedded fonts and full policy documents are not redistributed with facts.

The ordinary candidate publication is dated September 9, 2026. That printed
date remains separate from the later retrieval clock. Its 11 pages have SHA-256

## Fidelity and lifecycle

The standard bounded Poppler XML and SVG workers preserve page order, geometry,
English/Spanish instruction agreement, party columns, district labels, term
dates, explicit vote limits and cross-page continuations. Every nonspace native
character must match a reviewed visible glyph. Unknown fonts, altered glyphs,
hidden or clipped names, covering artwork and unconsumed fields require review.
The known partially clipped dash at the end of a Spanish office-heading line
does not relax clipping checks for names, dates, districts or parties.

The county's South Tucson instruction visibly says to vote for no more than two.
This source retains that instruction, without changing the older composite or
claiming that a vote limit establishes a seat count. Partisan codes remain
literal; `NON` for nonpartisan write-in offices is normalized to `NONPARTISAN`.
No nominee, winner, running mate or personal ballot is inferred.

Durable snapshots bind the complete offered document set and its fingerprints.
Removing an accepted publication or person holds the update and preserves the
last accepted data. Not-yet-published elections produce an awaiting-records
state, not a fabricated ballot. Public readers use stored facts without network
collection or native document conversion.

Synthetic future-year tests demonstrate the discovery and semantic contracts;
they are not factual election data. Actual source documents provide separate
native-worker and connected API/rendering verification. Source release and
receiving-host import/activation remain separate acceptance boundaries.
