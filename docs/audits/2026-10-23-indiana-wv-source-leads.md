# Indiana and West Virginia source-lead review

## Outcome

This cohort produces a research-lead CSV/JSON packet, not native ballot guides.
Seven named question/referendum leads concern Indiana and West Virginia; two
additional records distinguish South Carolina election preparation and a
Mississippi historical initiative archive from current measure qualification.
All nine records are explicitly discovery-only, noncanonical and unapproved for
publication or import. No question identifier, personal eligibility, final
ballot placement or absence of statewide measures is inferred.

## Indiana

The Election Division's current legislation index identifies a 2026 summary.
Official search discovery identifies two statewide public questions and three
county casino-referendum leads. A separate Department of Local Government
Finance language-review document identifies a Lebanon controlled-project lead.
Language review or a proposed-question receipt is not final ballot placement.
The county leads do not establish the identity of Allen County's third posted
question without permitted canonical evidence.

The exact HTTPS robots, election-agency homepage, policy index, legislation
index, terms and privacy paths return 200. The legacy portal root returns a tiny
HTML meta-refresh pointing to HTTP; it was not followed and did not grant policy
approval. The agency homepage was the independently existing repository source
reference and supplied a direct HTTPS policy link.

The terms limit personal use, derivative reuse and information-retrieval-system
incorporation, with a law-based exception whose applicability here is unresolved.
This is an access/reuse hold, not a legal conclusion about ownership of election
facts. Neither the summary nor a Clark County ballot hosted under that same
portal was downloaded. Cached web extraction is not accepted as canonical data.

Allen County independently publishes its own ballot-question page on a separate
county-operated site. Its robots request returns 403. No question image,
alternative vendor hostname, map portal or cached extraction is retrieved as a
workaround. Indiana's preceding candidate workbook and filing-status limits are
unchanged.

## West Virginia

Official discovery identifies a proposed citizenship amendment and an October 1
SOS explanation. No amendment number is invented. A future factual explanation
would need a permitted final text and the current-law comparison, including the
existing registration citizenship requirement; a title alone cannot establish
those effects.

The SOS robots and homepage return 200 with normal certificate validation. Its
linked statewide privacy/security/accessibility page returns 301, leaving that
review incomplete. No amendment document, sample PDF, mirror, cached extraction
or redirected policy is accepted. The separate candidate-host TLS failure is
neither fixed nor bypassed by the main SOS site's successful requests.

## Other discovery

South Carolina's current election-preparation page is not a current measure
certification. Mississippi's initiative index contains historical/expired items;
a separate 2026 election-logistics article does not qualify them for a ballot.
No sponsor contact details are retained, and neither result proves that the
state has no current statewide or local questions.

## Artifact boundary

The lead index, row-level CSV/JSONL, evidence receipts, required-evidence fields,
manifest and checksums remain distinct from native measure and coverage schemas.
Their candidate dates, scopes, titles and identifiers are research targets, not
voter-facing claims. The data-only packet contains no raw publisher PDF/HTML,
credentials or private operator prompt.

Existing native guides, candidate snapshots, active coverage, document-source
configurations and static UI are unchanged. No new automatic collector is
enabled. Source publication, import, private transfer, production actions,
scheduling and permission/configuration changes are outside this cohort.

## Verification

All nine CSV rows round-trip against JSON and JSONL without losing false
approval/canonical flags or required-evidence fields. Seven named leads and two
unverified-state discovery records remain distinct. The five archive members are
listed, independently extracted and byte-verified; raw publisher content is not
included.

Both source and compiled coverage parsers reject the lead index. Both source and
compiled native preparers reject it before creating output. All three targeted
release-data exclusion tests pass. The validation fixture is removed, and no
publisher request, active import or public route is exercised by these checks.

No application/runtime or page source changes occur, so this cohort does not
claim a new build or rendering acceptance run. The accepted native-guide
assembly remains the preceding Tennessee assembly rather than incorporating
research targets as questions.
