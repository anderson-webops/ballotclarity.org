# Chicago general-election document preparation

## Source

The independently published [Chicago candidate index](https://chicagoelections.gov/getting-ballot/candidates)
offers a full November 3, 2026 general-election document separately from a newer,
changing filing report. The full offered document, not that later filing report,
is the source reviewed here. Ordinary certificate-verified, public-IP-pinned
requests returned the city robots policy, candidate index and offered government
blob successfully. The linked blob host returned an ordinary empty XML 404 for
its absent robots file. The city footer and policy-link inventory were reviewed.
No alternate identity, blocked state-publisher source or challenge bypass was used.

The 28-page PDF has SHA-256
`d95940dbed40e6f95fb291a89a764eb93918b52f3cb9a61d720ae39af4f57b46`.
Its printed status date is September 11, 2026, at 3:47:15 p.m. This is not its
download time or proof of current certification. The source explicitly describes
unofficial candidate filings in ballot order for districts covering all or part
of Chicago.

## Prepared facts

The native reader prepares 201 ordinary candidate/ticket records and 60 separate
judicial-retention questions. The 261 records comprise 14 statewide, 20
congressional, 19 state-senate, 47 state-house and 161 local/judicial records.
Only printed executive pairings are combined. Parties, ballot numbers, selection
limits, full versus unexpired terms, judicial vacancies and converted judgeships
remain distinct. Blank write-in lines do not become candidates.

The last five pages supply 29 question occurrences with printed audience, title,
official wording, yes/no choices and page evidence. Identical wording in different
wards or precincts remains separate. Cook County, Chicago, school, service-area,
ward and precinct audiences are not interchangeable. An advisory label is retained
only when explicitly printed; voter applicability remains unverified. Neither
draft inventory nor party labels establish ballot qualification or personal
eligibility.

## Native and rendered fidelity

Bounded native XML is reconciled against visible SVG glyphs on every page.
Candidate and question text requires visible, correctly colored glyphs; opaque
overlays, unsupported graphics, missing glyphs and consequential clipping require
review. Zero-area spacing glyphs are not mistaken for visible missing text.
Limited clipping of the non-substantive introductory line and page-footer letters
is distinguished from clipping candidate or question facts.

The embedded fonts use changing subsets. Approval therefore binds Unicode
characters to reviewed TrueType glyph programs, composite dependencies and global
hinting tables, rather than pinning whole font files. Compatible subsets and
simple glyph renumbering remain reusable; altered character shapes, mappings,
composite programs and interpreter tables require review. The separate Arial
footer font is restricted to printed page labels. Source font binaries, PDF and
XML captures stay outside Git and release assets; source code contains hashes only.

## Integration boundary

This component does not enable a Chicago collection family or add public
coverage. The reusable collector still needs actual offered-date/link discovery,
current policy checks, explicit bounded worker capabilities, durable candidate
and question storage, revision/disappearance recovery and connected acceptance.
The printed source date must remain distinct from retrieval time. A Chicago-only
publication must not be selected merely from a Cook County match. Question
inventory is not a set of reviewed public measure pages.

The Illinois state-publisher hold remains independent. Current canonical data and
the pending data handoff remain unchanged by reader preparation.
