# Goffstown automatic ballot collection

## Publication and access

The town's [elections page](https://www.goffstownnh.gov/325/Elections) offers the
November 3, 2026 general-election sample separately from its primary and town
ballots. Discovery rechecks the exact reviewed robots rules, linked notices,
footer and legal-link inventory, then binds an explicit general-election date to
the document actually offered under that heading. Requests retain the existing
public-address-pinned transport, verified TLS and eleven-second spacing.

The [accepted two-page sample](https://www.goffstownnh.gov/DocumentCenter/View/6157/Goffstown-SAMPLE-BALLOT---State-General-Election-110326-PDF)
is 60,787 bytes, SHA-256
`740d817ffcffe002814ddf75f58301d107d3c9845045346eaf6cb4a454e33996`.
The October 27 source-agent attempt revalidated all five index/policy pages but
the PDF returned HTTP 302 to its exact own relative path. That redirect was not
followed. The earlier normal HTTP 200 capture remains valid evidence; replaying
those bytes is distinguished from a new live download and retains its original
retrieval time. This integration does not establish receiving-host access or
resolve the publisher's intermittent redirect.

The linked copyright notice reserves rights. Imports retain factual candidate
fields and official question wording with document/page attribution. No candidate
statements, font programs, source artwork or complete PDF is distributed in Git
or runtime release assets. Other offered primary and March PDFs were inspected
only for possible font calibration. Their candidates, results and eligibility are
not added to the general-election data; their different typography is not treated
as an interchangeable source.

## Native and visible agreement

The native text layer misencodes some printed quotation marks and splits some
names across fields. Its raw characters are therefore not accepted on their own.
The collector matches every nonspace native character to visible glyph ink and a
reviewed shape hash, with exact field geometry, color and visibility checks.
Both original pages conserve all 3,682 visible nonspace characters. PDF text,
Unicode and glyph changes must agree; invisible text, transformed glyphs,
unrecognized images, altered decoration and opaque overlays remain held.

Hash-only approvals cover the reviewed glyph shapes and fixed nontext structure.
Ballot ovals have independently validated shape and style, with positions matched
to candidate fields or the printed Yes/No choices. Their count can change without
silently merging names. Multi-line names map to one printed oval. Party labels
come from the actual columns or explicit other-party label, not inference.
Known quote corrections are applied only after visible-glyph validation.

New glyphs and layouts require review. In particular, synthetic date discovery
does not establish that every future font subset or date character is already
approved. This is a source-fidelity exception, not an annual URL or candidate-list
edit. The existing access and fidelity holds for the separate state, Bristol and
Alton sources remain intact.

## Accepted facts and applicability

The general sample yields 36 printed candidate/party claims and 35 distinct
candidate/office records. One person appears in both major-party columns. The
two House groups retain four- and two-seat limits and the county-named
`HILLSBOROUGH-29` and `HILLSBOROUGH-44` identifiers. Those labels are not converted
to bare Census districts. Unprinted congressional, Senate and regional district
numbers remain unknown. Listed, unopposed, qualified, elected and nominated are
not treated as equivalent statuses.

Two complete questions retain their actual choices and wording, including the
legislative passage note in the constitutional question. That note is not an
election result. Question numbers distinguish identities even if wording repeats.
Neither question claims verified voter applicability. The source is a sample,
not a personalized or complete town ballot.

The original three-town 76-record snapshot remains separate and unchanged. The
new source has lower default priority, so adding its automation does not hide the
broader reviewed directory. Its unresolved district scopes are withheld from
address-based ballot matching. Source records and question details remain
individually accessible through the existing public pages.

## Collection and recovery contract

The reusable family writes external election catalogs and data snapshots rather
than requiring election datasets in Git. It reuses the verified discovery capture
for import instead of downloading the PDF twice. Snapshot identity includes the
exact document digest; candidate, question, capture-time and provenance checks
survive durable restart. Public stored reads require neither native tools nor
publisher requests. A newly announced election with no offered sample is pending,
not an accepted empty roster.

Removing a previously accepted publication, candidate or question requires review.
Format, access, capability or persistence failures retain accepted files and
capture clocks. The existing exact-source allowlist, recovery copies and
publication/scheduling gates remain. Native collection uses the already supported
`STATE_BALLOT_PDFTOHTML` and `STATE_BALLOT_PDFTOCAIRO` capabilities; it introduces no
paid provider or private voter data.

The source workflow, local factual packet and receiving-host operation remain
distinct. Source publication alone does not demonstrate data receipt, installation
of scheduled collection or live activation. Nationwide future-election coverage
and the remaining independent state/local sources are still unfinished.
