# Wyoming homeowner initiative and source access

## Official scope

The [2026 election index](https://sos.wyo.gov/Elections/2026ElectionInformation.aspx)
links a five-page statewide proposition publication and eight-page voter guide.
They identify Proposed Initiative Proposition Number One and the exact printed
choices **FOR / AGAINST**. The index and rendered guide cover establish the
November 3 general-election date. This is a statutory initiative, not a newly
identified constitutional amendment or complete local-ballot inventory.

The proposal exempts half the assessed dwelling value of a qualified primary
home. Its text includes one-year state residency, six-month prior-year occupancy,
one exemption per owner/property each year, an initial sworn claim and subsequent
annual confirmation. The full five-page linked initiative was visually reviewed.
This does not establish a universal 50% reduction in an owner's final tax bill.

The voter guide's PDF page 3 says statewide propositions need approval by a
majority of all general-election voters. The draft retains this consequential
denominator rather than implying a majority only among question responses.

## Fiscal and current-law distinctions

The official ballot estimate projects state revenue decreases of **$92,614,266
in FY 2028** and **$95,855,766 in FY 2029**. It uses 2023 property data and a
3.5% annual growth assumption, and excludes local-government effects. The text
names tax year 2025, while the publication's explanation identifies 2027 as the
earliest applicable tax year after adoption in 2026, with state effects from 2028.
Both identities remain retained; the draft does not silently rewrite the bill.

An independently published Legislative Service Office packet has a memorandum
dated **May 20, 2026**, despite its June meeting filename. Selected pages 1, 5
and 42–45 were reviewed, not the entire 101-page packet. A dated fiscal-note
description distinguishes the existing 25% home/improved-land exemption from the
initiative's dwelling-assessed-value exemption. It supports an attributed
comparison, not independent verification of every current exemption or tax bill.

The packet also contains proposed HB147 text, its separate combined-repeal
scenario and a matrix that labels the initiative a constitutional amendment.
Those are not substituted for the certified ballot or classified as enacted
current law. Its $43 million/$43.9 million scenario uses 2025 data, a 2% growth
assumption and different school/local scope. The native fiscal cards preserve
the ballot publication's own figures and limitations instead of mixing estimates.
Independent current-law, exemption-stacking, applicability, updated fiscal,
rights and publication review remain open.
