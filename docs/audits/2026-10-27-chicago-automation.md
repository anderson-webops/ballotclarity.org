# Chicago general-election collection

## Publication and policy

The [Chicago candidate index](https://chicagoelections.gov/getting-ballot/candidates)
offers an explicitly dated full general-election list separately from changing
filing reports, lottery documents, calendars and candidate resources. Collection
uses only the full-list control's actual date and offered government-blob URL.
No filename, election identifier, nominee or future publication is guessed.

The city robots policy and normalized copyright/footer/legal-link inventory are
checked before collection. The independently offered blob host currently returns
an absent-robots HTTP 404; a new policy or blocked response requires review before
PDF retrieval. Requests use ordinary certificate-verified public-IP-pinned
transport without redirects, alternate identities or state-portal access. The
separate Illinois State Board of Elections hold remains unchanged.

## Current source and fidelity

The offered PDF remains the original 28-page publication, SHA-256
`d95940dbed40e6f95fb291a89a764eb93918b52f3cb9a61d720ae39af4f57b46`.
The complete worker reproduced all 261 prepared records and 29 question
occurrences exactly. These are 201 ordinary candidate/ticket rows and 60 individual
judicial-retention questions. Repeated question wording in different audiences
remains separate. Blank write-in lines do not become candidates. Candidate
filings remain unofficial; the printed September 11, 2026 status date is retained
separately from October retrieval and is visible on public source records.

Native XML, embedded-font character programs and every rendered page must
reconcile. Existing glyph approvals support compatible subsets rather than pinning
the entire font file. Unknown mappings, changed character programs, missing text,
unsupported graphics or consequential clipping remain review conditions. The
Chicago worker profile permits at most 50 native pages and 16 MiB of object output;
the standard worker remains limited to 20 pages and 4 MiB. PDF size, process time,
rendered-output and structural bounds remain intact. At most two page renderers
run concurrently. Font binaries and original publication captures remain outside
Git and public release assets.

## Durable collection and public reads

`il-chicago-2026` is an external-storage collection family. Future source identities
come from actual offered November general-election dates. An absent future list
does not generate fictitious candidates or a guessed URL. Accepted unexpired
elections, candidates and question occurrences remain recoverable when a list
disappears or parsing fails. A backward printed source date also requires review.
Unchanged publication digests retain original capture clocks.

Candidate metadata, printed source dates and audience-specific question wording,
choices and page evidence persist together. The Chicago-specific snapshot contract
retains vote limits and term lengths without relaxing the generic reviewed-PDF
contract or changing other collectors' question inventories. Public readers use
persisted facts without running converters or contacting publishers; later imports
become visible without restarting the reader.

The source's question index and individual question pages are linked from candidate
records. They show official wording and published audiences, not inferred fiscal
analysis, voting advice or personal applicability. Questions are not converted into
reviewed measure summaries automatically. An address-matched Census place may
prefer the Chicago source; Cook County, ZIP and IP matches alone do not. Local,
school, judicial, ward and precinct records remain unassigned without appropriate
district evidence.

## Acceptance boundary

Local collection, durable storage, API, rendering and source-release acceptance are
separate from production activation and the server's import/schedule acceptance.
Future-date examples in tests are synthetic. Unknown future layouts and policies
still require intervention rather than silently producing plausible ballot data.
