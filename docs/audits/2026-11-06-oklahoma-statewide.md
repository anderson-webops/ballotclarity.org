# Oklahoma statewide candidate collection

The State Election Board publishes a structured [election list](https://hosting.okelections.gov/electionlist.html)
with federal, statewide, legislative, judicial, county and municipal races.
`ok-seb-2026` adds an independent source alongside the existing reviewed Woodward
samples. Its recurring family discovers later even-year November general
elections from the publication's own heading and an actually offered official
[voter calendar](https://oklahoma.gov/elections/elections-results/next-election.html).

## Captured evidence

The November 2026 list contains 294 named candidate/office entries for 137 offices:
25 statewide, 14 congressional, 32 Senate, 111 House and 112 local. Its 2,576
candidate references span 77 county contexts. Local candidates occur in 40
counties. Browser reconciliation checked every candidate reference and all 1,870
office, retention and question headings against the original rendered HTML.
Thirty-one previously retained named Woodward candidates reconcile separately.

The file's original clock is September 24, 2026 at 8:39:12 AM, without a stated
time zone. November 6 captures preserve that date and their own acquisition
clocks. This is historical November 3 candidate information, not election results
or a claim that the publication includes every subsequent change.

Fifty-seven entries omit party labels. Eight candidate entries explicitly identify
unexpired terms. The reader preserves these distinctions without inferring party,
incumbency, winners, unopposed outcomes, remaining term length or voter eligibility.
Judicial-retention headings provide no names, and measure headings provide no full
questions; neither becomes an invented person or application measure. Woodward's
separate reviewed retention records remain intact.

## Automated boundaries

The collector checks the state robots policy, the hosting publisher's reviewed
empty robots 404, and the four linked state policy regions and link inventories
before candidate acquisition. Missing robots is recorded as absence rather than
an affirmative reuse license. The vendor link on the state copyright page
describes DMCA notice procedures; its unavailable page is not bypassed or treated
as reviewed permission. Collection remains subject to separately scoped host
approval and publisher-policy review.

Requests use normal TLS, exact hosts, bounded UTF-8 bodies, no redirect following
and at least twenty-second pacing. Changed policy, markup, scripts, county
inventory, calendar dates or conflicting office membership stops collection.
Candidate disappearance retains accepted data for review. Readers consume
persisted external snapshots without initiating this multi-request collector.

The rolling list can advance to a different month or disappear. An active
accepted election then holds for review; archived cohorts remain in the catalog
when a later general election becomes available. Actual calendar links are
followed rather than guessed year paths. The existing two-day post-election
worker cutoff remains in effect, while archived records stay readable.

Canonical facts, original captures and prepared CSVs remain external to Git and
immutable runtime assets. Source delivery, receipt, import, publication and
recurring scheduling retain their separate acceptance boundaries.
